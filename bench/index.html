K2FFT benchmark results

2026-11-07, the CI run.

Method

  • srof: single-precision, real-data, out-of-place, forward, 1d, at every 2^a 3^b 5^c, a >= 1, from 32 to the largest size K2FFT takes there (65536; 16384 on Cortex-M, 4096 on ESP32): every size it takes, its multiples of 32, and the others between.
  • mflops = 2.5 N log2 N / t[µs], as benchFFT: not an actual flop count, but a scaling that compares sizes on one graph.
  • Timed: FFTW's libbench2, one process a code, size and round, 5 rounds in alternating order, on one core; the plots show the median round. Busy: timed after 256 KB of other writes.
  • QEMU instruction counts: (N(3) - N(1)) / 2 instructions a transform, at 1 instruction = 1 ns. A board's cycle counts: the same, from its cycle counter.
  • No rival beat a code if none was faster in all rounds but one by more than the floor, both conditions; the floor is the larger of 3% and the widest pffft/pffft_twin spread; counts are one round with 15%.
  • Accuracy: libbench2's relative rms error, as benchFFT's. Setup time: libbench2's, planning included.
  • Plots as GFFT's (ICPP 2023) figures 10-14: K2FFT against FFTW, the vendor's library and the libraries no rival beat at some size. "vs FFTW 1.17x AVG.": K2FFT's speed over FFTW's, geometric mean over the panel's sizes; "fastest": the fastest rival at each size, K2FFT's own kernel (hwyfft) left out.

Caveats

  • Counts measure work, not time: QEMU doesn't emulate how long an instruction takes.
  • The macOS and Windows ARM64 runners are VMs; CI runners are shared, which the paired rounds and the floor answer.
  • Counts, cycles and times don't compare across tables, nor machines across rows.
  • oneMKL and IPP pick their code by CPU, and GitHub's x86-64 runners are mostly AMD EPYCs: the papers' oneMKL figures are from Intel Xeons, their AOCL-FFTW figures from an EPYC.
